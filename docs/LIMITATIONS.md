# Known limitations

- SEC standardized XBRL history generally begins in 2009. Pre-XBRL history requires a filing-document parser and company-specific validation.
- The built-in watchlist currently includes NVDA, AAPL, GOOGL, MSFT, META, V, MA, ANET, BKNG, NOW, SPGI, ABNB, CME, PAYX, IBKR, MSCI, VEEV, ZTS, CBOE, CPRT and FDS. Any exact ticker in the SEC registry can also be resolved on demand; the built-in profiles add verified split and listing metadata that a dynamic profile does not have.
- A dynamically resolved filer is economically classified from its official SEC SIC before industrial ROIC, FCFF or enterprise-value measures are considered. Verified CIK exceptions remain more precise for Berkshire, CME and Cboe. If the SEC classification cannot be read, the company load fails rather than silently treating a financial institution as an operating company.
- US-listed foreign private issuers that report only under IFRS remain outside the current statement adapter. The app returns an explicit IFRS limitation instead of drawing an empty or partly mapped company; supporting them requires a separate `ifrs-full` concept map and validation set.
- Some gaps are in the filings themselves and are reported rather than filled. Visa publishes neither a combined share count nor diluted earnings per share — both are tagged per share class, and the companyfacts endpoint carries only undimensioned facts — so its share count is recovered from the dividend it paid. That works from FY2022, the first year whose four quarterly dividend rates are all filed; before that the rate cannot be turned into a yearly total, so Visa's per-share series start there rather than being extrapolated. NVIDIA and Airbnb omit capital expenditures for some years, which removes free cash flow for those years only. Pre-IPO years computed under the two-class method fail the net income ÷ diluted EPS identity, so a share count is not recovered for them.
- Quarterly normalization depends on standardized SEC concepts. Unsupported company extensions stay unavailable; cumulative facts are differenced only when their fiscal starts and durations are compatible.
- TTM requires four consecutive reliable quarters totaling 330–380 days. A gap, overlap or missing metric produces no TTM value for that metric.
- The Overview does not amplify one missing source quarter into four long-range holes. Its 10Y and Max ranges use exact filed annual periods; 4Y uses quarterly TTM only when every Overview line that the filer reports is continuous, and otherwise falls back to annual. Charts still expose the quarterly and TTM series, including real source gaps.
- A deterministic 110-ticker SEC registry sweep on 1 September 2026 found internal annual gaps in revenue for 1 of 91 normalized companies, net income for 0 of 91, and free cash flow for 2 of 91. This is a measured normalization baseline, not a promise that every standardized concept exists for every issuer.
- Where a filer's derivable quarters stop, the current figures come from its latest annual report instead of from the last trailing window. JPMorgan's quarterly concepts stop lining up after 2014, and reading that window as "now" dated its whole company page to 2014 while today's price sat beside it. The later of the two periods wins, and the header names which one it is.
- Yahoo Finance pricing uses an unofficial public interface and can be rate-limited. FinScope fails closed and never replaces an unavailable historical price with the current quote.
- SEC focal-period share counts are normalized with the registry's effective split events; Yahoo adjusted close is split-adjusted. New companies need validated split metadata before long-range per-share comparisons are considered complete.
- Basic weighted-average shares, point-in-time shares outstanding and issuance proceeds may be missing when a filer does not use a supported standardized concept.
- Announced repurchase authorizations are narrative disclosures and require a filing-section parser. FinScope currently keeps reported repurchase cash flows separate from effective share-count changes.
- No forward-looking data of any kind. Next-twelve-month multiples, price targets, PEG and forward growth are analyst consensus estimates; FinScope reads filings and prices and has no estimates provider.
- Return on tangible assets is unavailable for filers that tag neither goodwill nor acquired intangibles, and interest coverage for those tagging no interest expense. Apple stopped tagging goodwill in 2017 and interest expense after 2023.
- Employee counts are not in the SEC companyfacts endpoint, so headcount statistics are absent.
- Non-GAAP metrics are intentionally excluded until a source-specific taxonomy and reconciliation model is implemented.
- Favorites and recent companies are device-session UI state in this build; there is no cloud persistence or account database.
- CSV, SVG, PNG and clipboard-table exports are implemented. Raster export uses the rendered chart and follows the active series configuration.
- A company outside the built-in list is normalized on demand. It is not covered by the cron, which walks that list only; instead the request that reads a reader's watchlist refreshes one aged company behind it, so a company they added stays current over a few visits rather than in one. Its first load takes a couple of seconds and can still be refused when the Worker is busy; loading it again succeeds.
- A company outside the built-in list carries no verified split history, so its long per-share *price* series are unadjusted. Its filings are adjusted exactly as any other filer's, because the SEC restates them.
- SEC ticker resolution follows the current registry CIK. When a public ticker moves to a newly created legal successor, that CIK can have only the successor's filings even though the predecessor has a long separate Company Facts history. FinScope does not join those entities without a validated lineage rule; the current Exxon Mobil registry mapping is a measured example.
- Market data for a company outside the built-in list is resolved by assuming the exchange ticker is also the Yahoo symbol. Where it is not, no sessions are found and the gap is reported rather than filled.
- The front page is prerendered and costs about a millisecond of Worker CPU. Any dynamic API in the root layout — `headers()`, `cookies()` — silently reverses that and makes every page view a full server render of the application tree; a regression test guards against it.
- Refilling the cache after a version bump is a paced job, not a fast one. One build every eighteen seconds ran for fifteen companies on 1 September 2026 and then met the CPU limit, after which every further request was refused for minutes — and a script that keeps knocking at the same interval never gets back in. `scripts/warm-cache.mjs` waits thirty seconds between builds and stands back three minutes after a refusal, which is what a full watchlist actually takes.
- Building a company from raw XBRL is expensive enough that a burst of cold builds gets the Worker throttled: eighteen inside ninety seconds, measured on 2026-08-29, refused every subsequent request within ten milliseconds of CPU for about four minutes — including the prerendered front page, which does almost no work of its own. Static assets and edge-cached API responses kept answering, so the site looked half up. Waiting clears it. The warm-up therefore paces itself, retries slowly, and rebuilds at most six aged companies per run; ordinary use never meets this.
- **Nothing is converted between currencies.** A filer reporting in euros while its shares are quoted in dollars — ASML — keeps its statements as filed, and every figure that would combine the two is withheld with that reason on the row: market capitalisation, enterprise value, all valuation multiples, the dividend yield, the DCF's price comparison and the reverse-DCF entry price. The filings themselves, and everything computed inside them, are unaffected.
- **Total debt is the most complete filed reading at that date, and the figure says which.** A filed all-debt total is used where one exists; otherwise the non-overlapping current, long-term and short-term balances are summed; otherwise a single filed long-term balance stands alone. Two concepts naming the same balance are never counted twice, and an absent balance is never read as zero. What a reading leaves out is stated on the figure — a lone long-term line does not include an untagged short-term facility, and a lone non-current line does not include current maturities. A sweep of 110 US filers on 1 September 2026 found 27% with no debt total under the previous stricter rule, including Meta, Home Depot, Caterpillar and McDonald's, none of which is debt-free; that is now 12%, and what remains is filers who tag no borrowing balance this adapter reads — Arista, Veeva, Palantir, Berkshire and Interactive Brokers among them — where the measures stay absent with their reason.
- **Market capitalisation may rest on the diluted weighted average.** A filer with several share classes tags its share count per class, and only undimensioned facts reach this endpoint, so no period-end count exists for Meta, Visa, CME, Interactive Brokers or Berkshire. The diluted average is used and labelled as such wherever the figure appears; it is an average over the period rather than a count on one day, and it differs from the true count by roughly 1.6% at Apple and 4.4% at Rivian.
- Provider availability and SEC taxonomy changes can create gaps. Errors are shown and the verified offline fixture remains available.
